The 5 Minute App Era: You Used to Need to Know Things to Make Stuff Online

Lately, I've felt an existential battle. It has to do with AI chatbots and their ability to generate any code in an instant. The code usually works. It's often better than I will ever be able to write.

I feel like I am standing at a crossroads, with so many options to move forward it is overwhelming. I used to get some satisfaction out of putting in the work to learn how to make something. If code for an app can be generated by prompting an LLM, what am I actually doing? I can make things faster than before. This is a nice change. But anything I can do is now more easily replicated by anyone with an idea and a chatbot. No struggle required. Just a bit of experimentation and the possibility of fixing AI hallucinations.

Once you have the code generated by an LLM, testing and revision begins. Coding experience comes in handy for this part of modern development. A good developer needs to have an eye for where the LLM may have gone astray. This is where the fundamentals of the coding language and knowledge of systems it runs on is required.

Nonetheless, I hypothesize we live in the age of the 5 minute app. It's easier than ever to crank out a minimum viable product. So why do I feel paralyzed from building? Is it the weight of knowing I could go in infinite tangents, building unlimited prototype products?

How do I know the idea is actually worth pursuing? Maybe in the age of the 5 minute app, I shouldn't worry about whether an idea is good or bad. Just execute the idea and see if it gets traction.

I spent thousands of hours and hours grinding to learn the fundamentals of Python. With the growth of AI code generation, it has left me feeling like all my effort didn't actually get me that much farther ahead. It feels like I peaked near 2020 or 2021 in terms of having an edge. Now, all my efforts over the years have been negated somewhat by the fact that any non-coder can take a chatbot and theoretically reach a working app. This would have taken me weeks or months to do 5-10 years ago.

The last mile of app development is where my grinding should give me an edge. The time spent learning the nuances of Python are helpful, but not exactly necessary in today. If anyone can create an app so easily, is there any real moat for something I create? This could be a reason to not worry about how good the idea is and just ship. Which is better: pumping out 5 apps and hoping one of them gets traction, or spending time to take the best idea and focus on that. Given we don't always know which is best, which strategy is best for app development? Unfortunately, this post has more questions than answers. I'm guessing the answer is simple, just ship something and see where it goes.
